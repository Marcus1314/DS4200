Why I love data + finance

I enjoy the crossover between data and finance because it balances logic with real-world impact. Data science gives me practical tools to clean messy datasets, build clear charts, and test simple hypotheses. Finance provides structure for thinking about risk, time, and value, which are things people face every day. When a question appears—“Is this trend meaningful or just noise?”, or “Which decision is safer for a family’s budget?”—a concise visualization and a well-explained metric can help. I like turning information into something readable so teammates and classmates can act quickly. That often means writing a short explanation, selecting one effective chart instead of five, and highlighting the main takeaway.

In my projects I try to follow three steps: understand the question, design the data pipeline, and communicate the result. For example, I practice documenting every transformation so my notebooks are reproducible. I also care about accessibility: larger labels, clear legends, and color choices that remain readable. You can see a small D3 demo on the Visualization page, where I summarize social-media likes by age group and plan to add a bar/line example. Long-term, I want to combine finance and data tools to help families invest more safely and reduce avoidable risks while keeping the story easy to understand.

Things I’m practicing

  • Clean, reproducible notebooks with comments
  • Picking chart types that match the question
  • Writing short, action-oriented summaries
